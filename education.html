Lux et Veritas PONO

Light, truth, and right relationship

Lux — Light — Transparency

Public records are easier to use when agendas, votes, contracts, and related documents are gathered in one place and linked back to their sources.

Veritas — Truth — Primary Sources

The record leads. When a source is available, compare a claim with the official minutes, filed document, or recorded vote before drawing a conclusion.

Pono — Righteousness — Right Relationship

Learning is stronger when it treats people, places, and evidence with care. Ask what the record says, whose perspective is missing, and what responsible participation looks like.

Learning activities by stage

These are adaptable activities built around public sources. They are not a claim of state or district approval; educators and organizations remain responsible for local curriculum, accessibility, privacy, consent, and safety review.

Ages 5 – 8 · Kindergarten through Grade 2

Our Community & the People Who Serve It

Young learners can discover that local government is made of people with jobs, decisions, and a connection to the places where they live.

Who helps run our community?

Use a civic record to identify one public role and one responsibility. Keep the focus concrete: parks, roads, water, schools, or a meeting place.

What happens at a meeting?

Look at an age-appropriate agenda title and ask what people may need to decide together. An adult can select the source and provide context.

Care for shared places

Connect a public decision to a familiar place and discuss who may be affected. Keep the learning rooted in observation, care, and verifiable information.

Sample Activity
Map a community responsibility

Choose one source record with an adult. Name the public role shown in the record and one responsibility connected to it. Draw or write one way that responsibility touches daily life.

Time: 20 minutes  ·  Materials: a selected public source, paper, and drawing or writing supplies  ·  Learning: community roles, observation, source awareness

Guiding question: How do people care for a shared community?

Public work becomes less abstract when learners can identify a person, a responsibility, and a source record. Keep the conversation respectful, local, and appropriate to the group.

Grades 3 - 5: Read a local decision

Choose a current agenda item, identify the decision-maker, and write two questions that a careful reader would ask before deciding what the item means. Use the source link, not a summary alone.

Find an agenda ->

Grades 6 - 8: Compare a claim with its record

Read a news claim or public statement alongside a linked primary source. Mark what is stated, what is supported by the source, and what still needs verification.

Open News vs Record ->

Grades 9 - 12: Trace a public decision

Follow one decision from agenda to meeting record to related documents. Discuss how a person can participate responsibly under school, district, and family guidance.

Open the civic briefing ->

College and university: Build a source trail

Use the public catalog to assemble a transparent research trail: source, date, jurisdiction, limitations, and the question the record can actually answer. Cite the original material in the final work.

Open data catalog ->

Graduate and professional: Test a policy question

Define a narrow question, document the source boundary, and state uncertainty plainly. Use records as evidence, not as a substitute for legal advice, due process, or institutional review.
